A. Message vs. tf
----------------
A1. Convert an incoming geometry_msgs::Quaternion quat_msg to the
    already-declared tf2::Quaternion quat_tf:

    tf2::fromMsg(quat_msg, quat_tf);

A2. Convert a tf2::Quaternion quat_tf to a newly declared
    geometry_msgs::Quaternion quat_msg:

    geometry_msgs::Quaternion quat_msg = tf2::toMsg(quat_tf);

A3. Read only the scalar component (w) of a tf2::Quaternion:

    quat_tf.getW();

    The w() accessor is also available. The scalar part w should not be
    confused with the quaternion's norm or rotation angle.

B. Conversion
-------------
B1. Extract the yaw angle in radians from tf2::Quaternion quat_t
    with one function call:

    double yaw = tf2::getYaw(quat_t);

B2. Convert geometry_msgs::Quaternion quat_msg to an Eigen 3x3
    rotation matrix (two conversion/method calls):

    Eigen::Quaterniond q_eigen;
    tf2::fromMsg(quat_msg, q_eigen);
    Eigen::Matrix3d R = q_eigen.toRotationMatrix();

    This assumes a valid unit quaternion. If its norm differs from one,
    reject a zero-norm input and normalize q_eigen before converting.
    ROS messages store fields as x, y, z, w, whereas Eigen's four-scalar
    constructor takes w, x, y, z; tf2::fromMsg performs the mapping.
